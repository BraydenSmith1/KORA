1. Executive Summary

The distributed energy resource (DER) management software market is experiencing rapid growth, driven by the global energy transition, declining renewable costs, and increasing grid decentralization. Within this market, mini-grid and microgrid optimization software represents a high-growth segment with acute customer pain points and limited incumbent solutions.

Key Findings

Market Size: - Global DER management software: €45 billion by 2030 (18% CAGR) - Mini-grid/microgrid segment: €8 billion by 2030 (32% CAGR) - African mini-grid software: €450 million by 2028 (45% CAGR)

Market Dynamics: - 35,000+ operational mini-grids globally, projected 200,000+ by 2030 - 70-80% of mini-grids suffer significant curtailment (>40%) - Software penetration currently <15%, projected 60%+ by 2030

Opportunity: - First-mover advantage in optimization-focused software - Hardware commoditization shifts value to software/intelligence - Strong alignment with climate finance and impact investment

KORA’s Positioning: - Target initial market: €25 million (Madagascar + Kenya) - Expand to: €450 million (Pan-African) - Long-term: €8 billion (Global microgrids)

2. Market Definition & Scope

2.1 What is a Mini-grid/Microgrid?

A mini-grid (or microgrid) is a localized electrical system that can operate independently from the main grid, typically comprising:

  • Generation: Solar PV, wind, small hydro, and/or diesel/gas backup
  • Storage: Battery systems (increasingly lithium-ion)
  • Distribution: Low/medium voltage network
  • Control: Energy management and metering systems
  • Customers: Residential, commercial, and institutional users

Size Range: | Category | Capacity | Customers | Typical Use Case | |———-|———-|———–|——————| | Pico-grid | <1 kW | 1-5 | Single building | | Nano-grid | 1-10 kW | 5-50 | Small village | | Mini-grid | 10-1,000 kW | 50-5,000 | Village/town | | Microgrid | 1-10 MW | 5,000-50,000 | Industrial/campus/island |

KORA’s Target: Mini-grids (10-1,000 kW) and small microgrids (1-5 MW)

2.2 Market Scope

This study covers the software and intelligence layer for mini-grids, specifically:

In Scope: - Energy management systems (EMS) - Dispatch optimization software - Demand response platforms - Forecasting and analytics - Remote monitoring and control - Customer billing and metering integration

Out of Scope: - Hardware (panels, batteries, inverters) - Physical installation services - Project development and financing - Grid-scale DERMS (>10 MW systems)

5.2 Key Market Trends

Trend 1: Shift from “Electrification” to “Optimization”

Early mini-grid deployments focused on basic access. As the sector matures, operators recognize that: - Curtailment is the primary economic challenge - Revenue optimization is key to sustainability - Software creates differentiation in commoditized hardware

Trend 2: Consolidation Among Operators

Year Top 10 Developers’ Share Trend
2020 25% Fragmented
2023 40% Consolidating
2026 55%+ Concentrated

Implication: Fewer, larger customers with sophisticated needs → enterprise sales motion

Trend 3: Hybrid AC/DC Systems

Traditional AC mini-grids being supplemented with DC microgrids for: - Electric vehicles (e-mobility) - Productive use (irrigation pumps, cold storage) - Data centers and telecom towers

Implication: More complex optimization required

Trend 4: Grid Interconnection

Many mini-grids connecting to weak national grids: - Buy power when grid available/cheap - Sell back during outages - Provide grid stabilization services

Implication: New revenue streams from grid services

Trend 5: Productive Use Focus

Donors and investors increasingly require: - Support for income-generating activities - Agricultural processing (milling, cold chain) - Manufacturing (welding, workshops) - Commercial services (internet cafes, phone charging)

Implication: Time-of-use pricing and load management critical
